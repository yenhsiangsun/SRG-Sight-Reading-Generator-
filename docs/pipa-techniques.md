# Pipa technique studies

Techniques decorate an already composed melody when expression practice is enabled. They do not replace rhythm, transpose melody, or introduce pitches outside the selected modal material/range. There is at most one marked technique per two-bar region, with a lower overall budget for beginners. Unsupported registers simply omit that technique.

- Five separated rays indicate a five-finger roll on one sustained upper-string note; the mark applies to that note's written duration.
- Straight brush arrows and arpeggio waves are disabled in new generation, engraving and playback. Legacy saved annotations remain readable without discarding their pitches or chords.
- Open-string double stops retain both pitches without displaying a fingering digit. Standard tuning is A2–D3–E3–A3.

Sources: [Moshe Denburg / VICO practical pipa guide, pp. 3, 5–6](https://www.atlasensemble.nl/assets/images/practical%20info/Pipa%20by%20Moshe%20Denburg%20VICO.pdf), [Patty Chan, Centre for Music Innovations](https://musinno.com/plucked-strings-pipa-%E7%90%B5%E7%90%B6/). The user's exam photos informed the choice of techniques and phrase roles; no exam melody is copied.

Playback approximates roll timing with repeated attacks using the existing pipa recordings. It does **not** contain separately recorded finger-roll samples. Legacy brush/arpeggio chords attack together. Microphone assessment removes techniques, continuous-roll metadata and chords from its displayed and scored melody.

## Interior holds and continuous rolls (2026-10-23)

The user accepted B79's occasional interior quarter/dotted-quarter hold after the B80 listening comparison. Complex rhythm keeps its existing density guards: at least 90% of planned attacks before optional rare 32nds, short-note share at least 55%, and no more than a ten-percentage-point reduction. This separate rhythm planner is unchanged by continuous-roll decoration.

A standalone roll still requires a quarter note or longer and at least 0.35 seconds at the selected tempo. In complex rhythm, at most one such roll per study may continue across the preceding musical pulse, covering 2–3 adjacent melody notes. For example, beat 3's sixteenth and dotted eighth can continue into beat 4's quarter, with all written onsets, durations and pitches retained. The whole gesture contains a quarter-or-longer anchor and lasts at least a quarter beat; the shorter members are not independent short rolls. Rests, chords, tuplets, staccato and other existing articulation on the preceding pulse block this extension.

Each member carries the same bar-local `pipaRollGroup` bounds. Saved-score validation checks the complete contiguous group, including its held anchor and pulse boundaries; orphan short-roll metadata is rejected. Following the user's correction, the notation places **one five-ray sign at the beginning** of the gesture. A solid range line with a downward right-end hook spans the gesture above the staff and indicates its continuation through the final note; intermediate and final beats do not repeat the sign. Standalone rolls keep their own sign.

The slur requested by the user is anchored to the first and last participating **notes**, independently of the technique range line. For a short single-voice group with all stems up it curves below the noteheads; down-stem and mixed-stem groups use an upward curve. An already engraved slur with the same anchors and side is reused. The rejected proposal that fixed the slur above the range bracket is not retained. Range and slur ink share the same SVG/PDF paths and are included in system/staff clearance checks. Playback retains the written pitch changes and continues the finger cycle across the group. One group counts as one sparse gesture; no extra rhythm or pitch event is generated. Simpler rhythm tiers retain their existing placement.

Placement references: [Dorico's slur conventions, printed p. 869](https://archive.steinberg.help/dorico_pro/v2/en/Dorico_Pro_2_Operation_Manual_en.pdf) and [slur endpoint positioning](https://www.steinberg.help/r/dorico-elements/6.1/en/dorico/topics/notation_reference/notation_reference_slurs/notation_reference_slurs_position_endpoint_c.html). [Samuel Wong Shengmiao's pipa guide, printed p. 32](https://www.atlasensemble.nl/assets/files/instruments/Pipa/Pipa%20by%20Samuel%20Wong%20Shengmiao.pdf) distinguishes right-hand technique symbols above the notes and bracketed continuation. These sources support separating the technique instruction from normal slur placement; they do not mean every sustained roll universally requires an extra slur.
